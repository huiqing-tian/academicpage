Research

My research focuses on how organizations and supply chains can become more sustainable. At the organizational level, I am interested in how organizations develop the capabilities to achieve and sustain improvements in supply chain sustainability performance. At the individual level, I examine how employees navigate trade-offs between economic, social, and environmental objectives in their everyday decision-making.

Research areas

  • Sustainable Supply Chain Management
  • Sustainable Supplier Selection
  • Behavioural Operations

Research methods

  • Behavioural Experiments
  • Qualitative Interviews
  • Statistical Analysis

Research in progress

Ethical Climate and Supplier Selection: Unit Price versus Sustainability Performance

Johnston, D., Rungtusanatham, M., Tian, H., & Ramsay, K.

Manuscript under review at the Journal of Operations Management

This project examines how organizational Ethical Climate influences supplier selection decisions involving trade-offs between unit price and sustainability performance for three different sustainability performance metrics.

  • supplier selection
  • behavioural experiment

Building Sustainable Public Procurement Capabilities: The Role of Organizational Sensemaking

Tian, H., & Johnston, D.

Manuscript in preparation (Target: Journal of Supply Chain Management)

This project explores how employees in public procurement settings engage in sensemaking to negotiate a shared understanding of sustainability and how these sensemaking processes shape the development of organizational capabilities for sustainable procurement.

  • public procurement
  • qualitative interviews

Psychological Distance, Action Identity, and Sustainability Trade-offs in Supplier Selection

Tian, H.

Dissertation project · Research in Progress

This project draws on Construal Level Theory and Action Identification Theory to examine whether different ways of framing procurement responsibilities and consequences influence purchasing professionals' willingness to prioritize supplier sustainability performance over unit price.

  • supplier selection
  • behavioural experiment

Assessing the Substantiveness of Supply Chain Forced Labour Risk Disclosures in Canada

Tian, H.

Dissertation project · Research in Progress

This project examines how organizations communicate their responses to forced labour risks under Canada's Fighting Against Forced Labour and Child Labour in Supply Chains Act. Using text analysis of corporate disclosure reports, the study aims to identify patterns of disclosure strategies and how they change over time.

  • sustainability disclosure
  • natural language processing
